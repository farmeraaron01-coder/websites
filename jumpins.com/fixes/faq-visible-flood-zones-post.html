Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage in San Diego?

No. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage entirely. Flood coverage requires a separate policy through the NFIP (federal program) or a private flood carrier.

Which San Diego neighborhoods are in high-risk FEMA flood zones?

Commonly affected areas include Mission Valley (San Diego River floodplain), the Chollas Creek corridor (Southcrest, Shelltown, Mountain View, Encanto), Mission Beach, Imperial Beach, the Tijuana River Valley, Sorrento Valley, Lakeside, Santee, Del Mar, and lowland parts of National City and Chula Vista. Check your exact address at msc.fema.gov — zone lines can split a single street.

When is flood insurance required by a mortgage lender?

If the property is in a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area (any A or V zone) and the mortgage is federally backed or from a federally regulated lender, flood insurance is required by federal law for the life of the loan. If you don't buy a policy, the lender will force-place a more expensive one.

Is private flood insurance better than the NFIP?

Often, but not always. The NFIP caps residential coverage at $250,000 for the dwelling and $100,000 for contents. Private flood carriers offer higher limits, replacement-cost contents, and sometimes lower premiums and shorter waiting periods. An independent agent can quote both and compare.

Is there a waiting period for flood insurance?

NFIP policies have a 30-day waiting period, which is waived when the policy is purchased in connection with a mortgage closing. Many private flood carriers have shorter waiting periods.
