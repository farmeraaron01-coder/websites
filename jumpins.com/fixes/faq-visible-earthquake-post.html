Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover earthquakes in California?

No. Earthquake damage is specifically excluded from standard California homeowners policies. To cover earthquake damage you need a separate earthquake insurance policy, available through the California Earthquake Authority (CEA) or private carriers.

How much does earthquake insurance cost in California?

A typical California homeowner pays roughly $800 to $3,000+ per year depending on location, home construction, deductible, and coverage limits. Homes near active fault lines or on soft soil are at the higher end.

Is private earthquake insurance better than the CEA?

Often, yes. Private carriers can offer deductibles as low as 2.5–5% of dwelling coverage versus the CEA's typical 10–25%, plus higher limits and broader personal property coverage. An independent agent representing both markets can run a real side-by-side comparison.

What deductible does California earthquake insurance have?

Earthquake deductibles are a percentage of your dwelling coverage, not a flat dollar amount. CEA policies typically run 5–25%. On a $600,000 home with a 15% deductible, you pay the first $90,000 of damage out of pocket.
