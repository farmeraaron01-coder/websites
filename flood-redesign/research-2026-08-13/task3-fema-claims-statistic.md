# FEMA / NFIP: "share of flood claims from outside high-risk flood zones" — primary-source verification

Compiled 2026-08-13. Only FEMA.gov, floodsmart.gov, agents.floodsmart.gov, and Wayback snapshots of those domains are treated as primary.

### Executive verdict

Yes — FEMA does currently publish a citable figure, but it publishes **two different numbers simultaneously**, so the choice of citation matters. The most current, most specific, and most defensible figure is on FEMA's consumer NFIP site: **"Over the past 10 years (2014 - 2024), nearly one-third of NFIP flood insurance claims (29%) came from areas located outside of current high-risk flood areas"** ([floodsmart.gov, What is My Flood Risk](https://www.floodsmart.gov/flood-zones-and-maps/what-is-my-flood-risk)) — this is the only version with both a named period and an exact percentage, and the same 2014–2024 "almost one-third" framing appears in FEMA's August 2025 real-estate brochure and July 2025 NFIP Media Toolkit. At the same time, FEMA still has live pages and current-linked PDFs saying **"about 40%"** (e.g. an [April 7, 2025 FEMA press release](https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20250407/fema-launches-direct-customer-flood-insurance-premium-quoting-tool) and the *Answers to Questions About the NFIP* brochure), **"more than 25 percent"** ([2024 Texas press release](https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20240711/flood-insurance-valuable-resource-texans)), **"About 25 percent"** ([FEMA FAQ](https://www.fema.gov/faq/low-risk-flood-zones)), and **"more than 20 percent"** ([FEMA blog](https://www.fema.gov/blog/facts-and-myths-about-flood-insurance)). None of the 20/25/40% versions state a period covered, and **none of the sources — including the 29% one — publishes a methodology note beyond floodsmart's one-line "Results displayed using census tract data."** Practical recommendation: cite the 29% / 2014–2024 floodsmart figure with the year range attached, and avoid the unsourced "40%" even though FEMA still prints it.

Everything below counts **claim counts**, not dollars paid, wherever FEMA says anything at all. No FEMA source found expresses this share in dollars.

Key drift findings:
1. The famous **"40%" was a 2015–2019 snapshot**, per FEMA's own TMAC report and its own agent-facing article — not a standing average, despite the words "on average" in the brochures.
2. The **same floodsmart URL** that today shows 29% (2014–2024) was showing **"More than 20% of flood claims come from outside of high-risk flood areas"** in a Wayback snapshot dated **2025-05-06** — so within roughly the last 15 months FEMA replaced a bare "more than 20%" with a period-labelled 29%. Note that Wayback's index for this URL only holds snapshots from May 2025 onward, so earlier versions of this specific page could not be retrieved.
3. FEMA has **not** removed the older figures — the 20%, 25%, and 40% versions are all still live on fema.gov/agents.floodsmart.gov today, which is why every number between 20% and 40% can be "sourced to FEMA."
4. No FEMA source at any date publishes methodology, a denominator definition, or a link to the underlying claims extract. The closest thing is floodsmart's one line: "Results displayed using census tract data." FEMA's underlying data is public via the [FIMA NFIP Redacted Claims dataset](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/fima-nfip-redacted-claims-v2), which contains a flood-zone field — so the agency's figure is independently reproducible in principle, but FEMA does not show its work.

### Suggested safe phrasing for an X-zone pitch

Using only the current, period-labelled FEMA figure:
> "According to FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program, from 2014 to 2024 nearly one-third of NFIP flood insurance claims (29%) came from areas located outside of current high-risk flood areas." — [floodsmart.gov, What is My Flood Risk](https://www.floodsmart.gov/flood-zones-and-maps/what-is-my-flood-risk)

Avoid "40%" (a 2015–2019 window FEMA still reprints without saying so) and avoid the bare "1 in 4" / "more than 20%" versions (legacy pages with pre-Risk Rating 2.0 pricing still attached).
