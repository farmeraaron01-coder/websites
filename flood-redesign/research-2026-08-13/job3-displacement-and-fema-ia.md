# Brief 3 — California displacement cost, duration, and FEMA IA
Grok 4.6, 14 Aug 2026. Archived verbatim in substance; my verification notes are
marked CFI. Every figure below is dated because rents and award caps change.

## Bottom line (Grok's own summary)
- A 2-3BR California displacement plausibly requires **$1,742–$4,602/month** at
  FY2026 HUD FMRs in the requested counties.
- The Zillow comparison does **not** consistently show HUD FMR is conservative.
- **No defensible California-wide published furnished/short-term premium** exists.
- **No reliable published median displacement period** for California residential
  floods. Enormous variation, substantial long tail.
- FEMA can pay rental assistance, but only after a qualifying presidential
  declaration and geographic designation, subject to household eligibility.
  **Rental assistance has no annual dollar cap** but normally cannot exceed 18
  months.
- Without an applicable declaration/designation there is **no IHP rental grant**.

### Grok pushed back on my framing — correctly
My brief asked for Zillow "so I can show the official figure is conservative."
That was a leading question and the data does not support it. Zillow is ABOVE the
2BR FMR in only 3 of 8 counties (San Joaquin, Santa Clara, Monterey) and BELOW
the 3BR FMR in all eight. HUD FMR and ZORI measure different rental populations
with different unit-size and utility assumptions.

**Do not publish "HUD figures are conservative."** Publish HUD as the official
baseline and Zillow as a dated general-market comparison with its limits stated.

### Furnished / short-term premium — NOT VERIFIED
No government, HUD, or university study establishes a statewide premium.
Commercial pages claim 10-30% or 40-75% without transparent California
methodology; Grok classifies these as industry marketing, unsuitable for a
licensed insurance publication. Use dated local listings instead of a percentage.

## Job 2 — Displacement duration: no usable median
Studies define displacement incompatibly (evacuation order, neighbourhood
reentry, time living elsewhere, repair completion, permanent recovery).

- **NIST, Lumberton NC 2016** (SP 1230-2, Table 3-4): among displaced
  single-family households that had ALREADY RETURNED, mean **86 days**, median
  **7 days**; owners mean 93 / median 14. Excludes households still displaced at
  survey — the mean far exceeding the median demonstrates the long tail.
- **Colorado 2013** (Natural Hazards Center): Lyons evacuated 6+ weeks; ~145
  households (~15% of population) still displaced ~5 months on; some in limbo at
  9 months.
- **Hull, England 2007** (UKHSA): 59% of flooded households displaced; 12% over
  one year; a further 5% over two years.

### California events
| Event | Observation | Type | Limits |

| Russian River Jan 2017 | 550+ homes/businesses flooded | Journalism (KQED) | Scale only |

**Publishable conclusion:** show the cost of ONE MONTH, state that displacement
ranges from days to more than a year, and let the reader multiply. A single
"typical months displaced" figure would overstate the evidence.

## Job 3 — FEMA Individual Assistance
FEMA IAPPG (July 2025 amendment):
> "FEMA may provide financial assistance to pre-disaster homeowners or renters to
> rent alternate temporary housing..."
and
> "IHP assistance is not a substitute for insurance..."

Calculated from HUD FMR for the pre-disaster residence and bedroom count. Covers
rent and essential utilities; excludes telephone, cable, television, internet.
Initial award generally **two months**; continuation requires documentation of
continuing need, proper use of prior assistance, and a permanent-housing plan.

### Prerequisites
1. Presidential major-disaster declaration; 2. IA/IHP authorised; 3. the
applicant's county designated for it; 4. household eligibility.
Technical exception: IHP may be authorised under a presidential EMERGENCY
declaration, which FEMA describes as rare.

### CAPS — the correction that matters
**Rental Assistance, Lodging Expense Reimbursement and Direct Housing carry "No
Financial Maximum."** The annual IHP cap applies to Home Repair/Replacement and
Other Needs Assistance, NOT to rental assistance. The $43,600 figure (declarations
on/after 1 Oct 2024, Federal Register 2024-24700) must never be presented as a cap
on rental assistance. FY2026 maximum: **not verified**.
Duration: normally up to **18 months** from declaration, extensions possible, plus
one additional month for a security deposit.

### Without a declaration
> Without an applicable federal declaration and Individual Assistance designation,
> FEMA does not provide the household an IHP rental-assistance grant.

Grok's caution: avoid the unqualified "FEMA gives you nothing" — FEMA may support
emergency sheltering indirectly, and an NFIP building/contents claim is separate.

### CFI VERIFICATION — independently confirmed, 14 Aug 2026
Queried OpenFEMA DisasterDeclarationsSummaries v2 directly:
`state eq 'CA' and declarationType eq 'DR' and ihProgramDeclared eq true and
declarationDate ge '2016-08-13'` → 138 rows, **13 unique declarations**.

Flood-titled, with IA — exactly the four Grok listed:
- **DR-4353** 2018-01-02 Wildfires, Flooding, Mudflows and Debris Flows (4 areas)
- **DR-4683** 2023-01-14 Severe Winter Storms, Flooding, Landslides, Mudslides (15)
- **DR-4699** 2023-04-03 Severe Winter Storms, Straight-Line Winds, Flooding (14)
- **DR-4758** 2024-02-19 Severe Storm and Flooding (1 area — San Diego)

**A stronger fact than the one I asked for.** Of the 13 California declarations
carrying Individual Assistance in ten years, **eight are wildfires** and one is
COVID-19. Only four mention flooding at all, and one of those four (DR-4758)
designated a single county. Federal help for a flood displacement in California is
not the norm — it is roughly a once-every-two-to-three-years event covering a
minority of counties.

Grok's counterexample also checks out: **DR-4434** (Feb 2019 Guerneville/Russian
River) records Individual Assistance as **"not requested"** in FEMA's preliminary
damage assessment — a real California flood, no household IA at all.

## Does FEMA weaken our argument?
It kills the absolute claim "FEMA never helps with temporary housing." It does not
touch the gap:
- NFIP does not include ALE.
- FEMA rental assistance needs a declaration AND local designation.
- Eligibility, primary-residence status, insurance duplication, documentation and
  continuing need all still apply.
- Initial assistance generally two months.
- Amounts are HUD FMR-based and may not secure a furnished short-term house.
- Only three or four California flood disasters in ten years carried household IA.

**Supportable formulation:**
> The NFIP does not cover additional living expenses. FEMA rental assistance may be
> available after certain presidentially declared disasters, but it is not
> automatic and is unavailable when the household's area is not designated for
> Individual Assistance.
