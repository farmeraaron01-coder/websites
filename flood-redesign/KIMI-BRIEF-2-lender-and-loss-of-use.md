## Job 1 — The statutory definition of "private flood insurance" (small, precise)

Federal regulators require lenders to accept private flood insurance that meets a
statutory definition. I need **the definition itself, verbatim**.

Find **42 U.S.C. § 4012a(b)(7)** — the definition of "private flood insurance" —
and give me:

- The **full text of the definition, quoted exactly**, with its subparagraphs
- A direct URL to a primary source: uscode.house.gov, govinfo.gov, law.cornell.edu,
  or the eCFR text of the implementing regulation (12 CFR 22.2 / 208.25 / 339.2 /
  760.2 — whichever you can reach)
- Specifically: **does it use the phrase "at least as broad as"?** Quote the exact
  sentence containing it and tell me what it is comparing — the coverage provided
  by a standard flood insurance policy under the NFIP, or something else
- Whether the definition also requires anything about deductibles, exclusions, or
  cancellation provisions — quote those parts too

Then, separately: the 2019 interagency final rule contains a **compliance aid**
letting a lender accept a policy without further review if it carries a specific
sentence. Confirm **the exact wording of that sentence** and where the rule states
it, with a URL.

I have already confirmed the mandatory-acceptance rule itself (FDIC FIL-19-2019,
OCC Bulletin 2019-8, Federal Reserve, Federal Register 20 Feb 2019). What I do not
yet have is the definition's own wording. Do not paraphrase it — I need to know
whether specific words appear, because I intend to use them.

## Job 2 — Does anyone argue loss of use? (the bigger job)

Background: NFIP flood policies do **not** cover temporary housing or additional
living expenses. FEMA says so on floodsmart.gov. Many private flood policies do
offer that coverage. I think this is a stronger argument than price, and I want to
know whether anyone else is already making it.

**Survey the pages that rank for these queries:**

1. `private flood insurance vs nfip`
2. `does flood insurance cover temporary housing`
3. `does flood insurance cover living expenses`
4. `nfip vs private flood insurance california`
5. `loss of use flood insurance`

For **each ranking page** in the top 10:

- URL, site name, and type (carrier, broker, aggregator, government, news)
- **Does it mention loss of use / additional living expenses / temporary housing at
  all?** Quote the exact sentence if so.
- If it does: does it make clear the NFIP **does not offer it at all**, or does it
  fudge that? Quote exactly.
- Where does it sit on the page — headline, a comparison table, or buried in an
  FAQ?
- Does the page compare private and NFIP on any other coverage dimension? List
  what it compares and quote the figures or claims.
- Does the page mention lender acceptance of private flood, and how?

**Then tell me plainly:**

- How many of the pages you looked at make the loss-of-use argument prominently?
- Does anyone quantify it — an actual dollar figure or limit for loss of use
  coverage, or a worked example of months of displacement?
- Is there an obvious gap here, the way there was with flood-zone pricing?

If almost nobody makes this argument, say so clearly. That is the finding I am
hoping for, and a clean negative is as useful as a list.
