## What I want from you

I have a content plan. **I want you to try to break it**, not improve it.

I am a licensed California insurance agent. My site publishes flood insurance
costs. If this plan is wrong, I spend months building the wrong thing, or worse, I
publish numbers that get me in trouble with a regulator. A flaw you find now is
worth vastly more to me than encouragement.

Tell me plainly if you think the plan is sound. But look for the failure first.

## Specific things to attack

**1. Is the shape wrong?**

I concluded that one strong page with a full county data table beats 58 individual
county pages, mainly because I already have five near-duplicate pages competing
with each other and a contact page that was outranking my homepage.

- Is that reasoning right, or am I over-correcting from one bad experience?
- Are there sites in insurance, mortgages, utilities or similar that **have**
  successfully built large per-location page sets? What did they do that made it
  work? Give me actual examples with URLs.
- Is the opposite true — that in local-intent verticals, the per-location page set
  is the thing that wins and a single table is invisible?

**2. The data/demand mismatch — the thing I am most worried about**

My own premium data is strongest in Marin, Orange, Los Angeles, Santa Clara. It is
weak in Sacramento, which is the county most associated with California flooding.

- Roughly where **is** the search demand for California flood insurance by county?
  Anything you can find on relative search interest — Google Trends by metro,
  keyword tools, or the volume of competing content per county — helps.
- If demand really concentrates in the Central Valley and my unique data is on the
  coast, what is the right move? Write the page anyway with public data only? Skip
  it? Something else?

**3. Am I being paranoid about scaled content?**

I refused to build 58 near-identical pages partly because of Google's
scaled-content-abuse policy.

- Quote me what the policy **actually says**, from Google's own documentation, with
  a URL. Not a summary from an SEO blog.
- Does it apply to genuinely data-differentiated pages, or only to spun text?
- Has anyone in a regulated vertical been visibly hit for this? Examples.

**4. The suppression floor**

I refuse to publish a premium figure for any county with fewer than 10 policies
behind it, and I will not estimate or interpolate to fill gaps.

- Is n≥10 defensible as a threshold for publishing a median, or should it be
  higher for a public figure someone might rely on?
- Is there a recognised standard for minimum cell size in published statistics
  that I should be following instead of a number I picked?

**5. What have I missed entirely?**

The plan has three tiers: fix internal linking, build one flagship page, then build
a shortlist of county pages. What is not in it that should be? Be specific.

## Ground rules

- **Quote exactly, with URLs.** I check everything and I have caught errors in
  previous reports.
- Say **"not verified"** rather than giving a plausible answer.
- Do not estimate insurance premiums — I have my own data.
- **Disagreeing with me is the useful outcome.** If you think the whole plan is
