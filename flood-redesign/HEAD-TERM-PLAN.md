# `flood insurance california` — the plan, and what is actually wrong

Written 13 Aug 2026. Prompted by the `www` property export: **4,076 impressions, 24 clicks, average position
10.6** on that one query — the largest single impression pool in the export.

**First, the honest caveat on that number.** It came from the `http://www…` URL-prefix property, which covers the
homepage only. **The site's real position for this query is not yet known.** Step 0 below fixes that. Everything
here is calibrated to be right either way, because the defects found are defects regardless of position.

---

## The diagnosis: this is not a content problem

Five pages carry 3,000–3,900 words each, all titled some variation of "California Flood Insurance …":

| URL | Words | Title | Internal links in |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/` | 3,546 | California Flood Insurance: Save 30–50% on Flood Quotes | 124 |
| `/california-flood-insurance-quotes/` | 3,885 | California Flood Insurance Quotes: Compare & Save | **2** |
| `/cheap-flood-insurance-california/` | 3,730 | Cheap Flood Insurance California: Save the Smart Way | **2** |
| `/flood-insurance-rates/` | 3,828 | California Flood Insurance Rates: 2026 Pricing Guide | 66 |
| `/residential/` | 2,903 | Residential Flood Insurance in California: NFIP & Private | 234 |
| **`/contact-us/`** | **2,522** | **Contact California Flood Insurance \| 855-225-3566** | **123** |

### Finding 1 — the contact page is competing for the head term, and winning

A live search for `flood insurance california` returns **two** of our URLs: `/contact-us/` **above** the homepage.

That is the worst available outcome. Someone researching flood insurance lands on a contact form — no cost
figures, no NFIP-versus-private comparison, nothing that answers the query. It is a conversion dead end for
research intent, and it consumes the ranking the real page should hold.

**Why it happens is mechanical, not mysterious.** `/contact-us/` carries **2,522 words, 21 mentions of "flood
insurance", a title stuffed with the exact phrase, and 123 inbound internal links.** The site is telling Google
that its most important flood-insurance-in-California page after the homepage is the contact page. Google
believed it.

### Finding 2 — the two pages built for these keywords are orphaned

`/california-flood-insurance-quotes/` and `/cheap-flood-insurance-california/` have **2 inbound internal links
each.** Nearly 7,600 words of purpose-built commercial content with no internal support, while the contact page
gets 123 links and the quote CTA gets 534.

**This is the single largest lever on the page and it costs nothing but editing links.**

### Finding 3 — five near-duplicates split the signal

Every one of those pages is a substantial, commercially-intended page about buying flood insurance in
California. Google has five plausible candidates on one site and no clear winner, so it picks whichever page
happens to look strongest per query — which is how a contact page ends up ranking.

### Finding 4 — the homepage H1 contains none of the phrase

> `California floods. Your premium shouldn't.`

That is good copy and I do not want to lose it. But the H1 — one of the strongest on-page signals — says nothing
about flood insurance. The 11 Aug external audit flagged the same thing and was right to say **test** it rather
than assume.

## What the SERP says about intent and about the ceiling

Who currently holds that first page:

- **floodsmart.gov** (FEMA / NFIP)
- **insurance.ca.gov** (California Department of Insurance)
- **pwip.oc.gov** (Orange County)
- **LendingTree** — national aggregator, ranking on **county-level cost data**
- **katzinsured.com** — a small agency, which matters: agency sites *can* compete here
- `californiafloodinsurance.com/contact-us/` and `californiafloodinsurance.com/`

**Two conclusions.**

**The intent is cost and explanation, not purchase.** Nobody buys on this query. The pages that win it answer
"what does this cost and how does it work." LendingTree ranks with generic county averages — and **the one thing
an aggregator cannot copy is real placement data from an actual book of business.** Same play that makes the
sober living cost page worth building.

**Be honest about the ceiling.** Two government domains and the state regulator hold that page. **Positions 1–3
are not a realistic target.** Getting into the **5–7 range is realistic**, and that is where click share changes
materially — from well under 1% to several percent.

## The plan, in order of leverage per hour

### Step 0 — one report, before touching anything (Aaron, 10 minutes)

Search Console → **`californiafloodinsurance.com` Domain property** → Performance → 3 months →
filter **Query = `flood insurance california`** → **Pages** tab.

That gives which URLs rank, their real positions, and the click split. It confirms or corrects the SERP read
above and tells us whether `/california-flood-insurance-quotes/` earns anything on its own — which decides
whether it gets differentiated or redirected. **Do not redirect any page before seeing this.**

### Step 1 — stop the contact page competing (30 minutes, zero risk, biggest immediate win)

- **Move the flood-insurance content off it.** A contact page needs a form, phone, hours, address and a map.
  2,522 words about flood insurance is a content page wearing a contact page's URL. Relocate that material to
  whichever page Step 0 says should own the term.
- **Retitle plainly**, e.g. `Contact Us — California Flood Insurance Services · 855-225-3566`. The brand name
  unavoidably contains the phrase, and that is fine; what goes is the *topical* optimisation.
- **Keep the 123 internal links.** Footer and nav links to a contact page are correct. It is the topical signal
  that has to go, not the links.

### Step 2 — pick one owner and point the internal links at it (highest leverage overall)

**Recommended owner: the homepage**, unless Step 0 shows otherwise. It already ranks, holds the most authority,
and the query is broad.

Then add **contextual** internal links from the eight city pages, four flood-zone pages and the guides, using
descriptive anchors that vary naturally rather than repeating one string. Contextual in-body links carry more
weight than another footer entry.

`/california-flood-insurance-quotes/` is the closest duplicate of the homepage's job. If Step 0 shows it earning
little on its own, redirecting it into the homepage concentrates the signal. **If it earns long-tail traffic,
differentiate instead of merging** — losing long-tail to chase one head term is a bad trade.

### Step 3 — match the intent with data an aggregator cannot copy

Whichever page owns the term needs **real cost figures with a stated methodology** — ranges from actual
placements, by zone and property type. LendingTree is beatable here precisely because its numbers are generic
county averages. This overlaps the cost-page work already queued for the sober living site, and the same rule
applies: **no invented figures**, only ranges that can be stood behind.

### Step 4 — test the homepage H1, do not assume it

Get the phrase into the H1 while keeping the hook — the brand line can remain as a visible kicker above it, or
the H1 can carry both. **Run it as a test over 6–8 weeks** and watch position *and* quote-form starts. The
existing line is doing conversion work; a keyword win that costs conversions on the homepage is a loss.

### Step 5 — external authority, the slow part

Nothing above buys authority, and authority is what separates position 5 from position 2 on a commercial head
term. Realistic routes: the CDI/NAIC license profile, floodplain-manager and realtor associations, local press
on California flood events, and genuinely citable first-party data from Step 3.

---

## Expectations, stated plainly

- **Steps 1 and 2 are free, low-risk, and reversible**, and they fix signal confusion rather than authority — so
  they can move things within weeks rather than months.
- **Page 1 is a realistic goal. Top 3 is not**, against FEMA and the state regulator.
- **The business case is not the clicks alone.** Google CPC is already 1,631 sessions of 3,938 (48% of traffic is
  paid). Every organic position gained on a term that is currently bought reduces dependence on paid clicks for
  the same intent.

## Review of the Kimi report, 13 Aug — one excellent idea, and a false centrepiece

Aaron relayed a second external analysis. **Its headline finding does not survive checking, and its best idea is
better than anything in this document.** Both matter, so both are recorded.

### VERIFIED FALSE — the "three broken title tags"

It called this "the smoking gun." All three pages were fetched live, cache-busted:

| URL | Claimed title | **Actual title** |
|---|---|---|
| `/which-flood-zone-requires-flood-insurance/` | "Frequently Asked Questions" | **Which Flood Zone Requires Flood Insurance? (CA Guide)** |
| `/navigating-flood-zone-x/` | "How Much Does Flood…" (wrong page) | **Flood Zone X: Do You Need Flood Insurance in California? (2026)** |
| `/residential/` | "Residential" | **Residential Flood Insurance in California: NFIP & Private** |

**Every title is correct, specific and well-formed.** The estimate built on top of this — "roughly 30–50 extra
clicks per quarter" — has nothing under it.

**The tell was in the diagnosis, not the data:** it attributed the fault to *"a Divi/Yoast template variable
misfiring."* **This site runs Kadence and Rank Math.** Divi was removed and Yoast was never installed. An agent
that infers the stack rather than reading it is inferring the rest too.

### VERIFIED FALSE — AI crawlers being blocked, and robots.txt not responding

`robots.txt` returns **200 to GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and curl**, and its only rules are `/wp-admin/`
and the claim PDFs. Nothing is blocked. `sitemap_index.xml` also returns 200 to all four.

### ALREADY FIXED — the "junk in the index"

`/mobile/contact.php`, `/tag/nfip/`, `/tag/flood-insurance/` and `/residential-flood-insurance/` **all return 404
with `noindex`.** Those are Divi-era URLs; the impressions it saw are Search Console's 90-day history, not live
pages. No action.

### CORROBORATED — three things it got right

- **`wp-includes/` and `wp-content/` paths indexed.** Correct, and the cause and fix are in
  `california-hardening.conf`. Independently found the same day.
- **`http://www` indexed separately.** Correct, and now quantified in `WWW-AUDIT.md` — though its figure of 1,245
  impressions does not match the actual export's **15,479**, so use the export.
- **"robots.txt and sitemap didn't respond to my crawler."** Half right, and worth the credit: **the sitemap was
  genuinely returning 404** for part of 13 Aug after a theme replace dropped the rewrite rule. It may have crawled
  inside that window. `robots.txt` was never affected.

### THE ONE THING WORTH MORE THAN THIS WHOLE DOCUMENT — address-level queries

It reports hundreds of impressions on queries like `525 b street san diego fema flood zone` (68 impressions,
position 9.1) and `9725 scranton road san diego flood zone` (~68, ~9.9), with the **city pages** ranking for them.

**If those numbers are real, this is the best opportunity on the site and neither this document nor the 11 Aug
audit spotted it.** The intent is unbeatable: somebody typing a street address plus "flood zone" is an escrow
officer, a lender or a buyer **mid-transaction**, needing an answer today. That is not research traffic, it is a
deal in progress.

A **flood zone lookup by address** — or address-level modules on the city pages — would own a cluster nobody
else is serving, and it fits the business rather than just the ranking. **Verify the query data first**, then
treat it as a product decision, not an SEO task.

### Unverifiable from here — the per-page table

Its page-level impressions and positions cannot be checked without the **apex** property export; the export we
have is the `www` property, which covers the homepage only. Its numbers may well be right, and if so the table is
genuinely useful — it names `/how-much-does-flood-insurance-cost/` (2,579 impressions, position 17.8) as the
biggest single opportunity, and flags `/residential/` at 38.1 and striking-distance zone pages.

**One export settles both its table and Step 0 of this plan**: Domain property → Performance → 3 months →
Pages tab, plus the same filtered to `flood insurance california`.

### Where the two plans agree, and what each missed

**Agreed:** real cost data from the book of business is the highest-value content asset, internal links to
striking-distance pages are cheap and effective, and the cost page deserves priority.

**It missed** the cannibalization entirely — that `/contact-us/` outranks the homepage for the head term, and
that the two keyword-named pages have 2 internal links each.

**This document missed** address-level queries.

**The rule this produces:** an external page-level claim is worth exactly one `curl` before anyone acts on it.
Query-level claims cannot be checked that way, so they need the export.
