## el-nino-california-flood-risk.html

Published to California 5 Aug 2026 as `/el-nino-california-flood-risk/`. 1,367 words,
7 H2s, 5 FAQ pairs (the theme converts these to FAQPage schema automatically), 5 internal
links, all verified 200.

**Every figure is sourced, and two were corrected during drafting:**

- NOAA CPC ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, 9 July 2026 — El Niño Advisory in effect, Niño-3.4
  at **+1.2°C**, 97% chance of persistence into early spring 2027, **81% chance of a very
  strong event during October–December**, which would rank among the largest since 1950.
  A web search summary reported +0.7°C and a 63% chance; the primary source says +1.2°C and
  81%. Read the CPC discussion, not the summaries.
- Southern California sits in its wettest tercile in **8 of 10** moderate-to-strong El Niños,
  and none of those ten put Northern California in its driest tercile. This is why the
  article is framed around Southern California rather than "the west coast" — the Northern
  California signal is weak, and overstating it is the most common error in El Niño coverage.
- **2015–16 busted.** Ocean anomalies above 2°C, comparable to 1982–83 and 1997–98, and
  California still came in below an average non-El Niño year. Scripps traced it to Indian
  Ocean and western Pacific SSTs unrelated to El Niño. The article leads with this rather
  than burying it, because a licensed agency claiming a guaranteed wet winter is the version
  of this article that damages credibility.
- FEMA FloodSmart — *"From 2014 - 2024, nearly one-third of NFIP claims came from outside
  high-risk flood areas."* Used over the looser "about 40%" that circulates in secondary
  sources.

**Perishability.** ENSO outlooks update on the second Thursday monthly. The slug carries no
year so the URL survives revision; the year lives in the title and a closing note states the
advisory date. Update the figures rather than publishing a second article.
