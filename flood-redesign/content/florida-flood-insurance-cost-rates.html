Florida's Unique Flood Risk

Florida has three flood threats that stack: storm surge from hurricanes, riverine flooding, and heavy rainfall with poor drainage (high water table, flat terrain). This is why 60% of Florida homeowners have mandatory coverage.

Mandatory Zones (Your Lender Requires Coverage)

  • Zone VE: Very high risk, coastal surge. Most expensive.
  • Zone AE: High risk, riverine or storm surge dependent areas.
  • Zone A: High risk inland.
  • Zone AO: Shallow flooding (1-3 feet), usually coastal.

Optional Zones (But Don't Assume You're Safe)

  • Zone X: Low/moderate risk. But 29% of claims come from X zones nationally. Florida's water table and drainage patterns make this worth reconsidering.

FAQs

Do I really need flood insurance in Florida?
If you're in a mandatory zone, your lender requires it — no choice. If you're in a low-risk zone, it's optional but worth a conversation. Florida's terrain and rainfall patterns create flooding outside the official zones. We've paid six-figure claims on Zone X properties.

Why does coastal (VE) flood insurance cost so much?
Because hurricane surge is a real, modeled risk with high loss exposure. That said, we shop the private markets aggressively on VE properties — sometimes they compete better than you'd expect.

Can I get coverage for my home if it's been damaged before?
Yes — we have private programs that write homes with prior claims. The NFIP typically non-renews after a claim; private is where those policies go.
