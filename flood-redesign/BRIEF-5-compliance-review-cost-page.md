## What this is

I am a licensed California insurance agent (CA Lic. #0L75450). I have just
published a page that puts **premium figures** in front of the public:

**https://californiafloodinsurance.com/how-much-does-flood-insurance-cost/**

Read the live page. It is short enough to read in full, and I want you responding
to what is actually there rather than to my description of it.

**I am not asking whether it is good.** I am asking whether it is *safe* — and if
it is not, exactly which sentence is the problem.

A previous reviewer raised California Insurance Code § 790.03(b) as the operative
constraint on this kind of publication. **I have not read that section myself and
I am not relying on it until someone quotes it to me from a primary source.** That
is job 1.

## Job 1 — What does California law actually require of me here?

- Quote **Cal. Ins. Code § 790.03(b)** verbatim from a primary source
  (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov). What conduct does it actually prohibit?
- Does publishing **historical premium statistics** — clearly labelled as
  historical, with sample size and date — fall inside or outside that prohibition?
- What are the **penalties**, and do they attach per publication, per viewer, or
  per complaint?
- **Which advertising regulations apply to property & casualty?** A previous
  report quoted 10 CCR § 2547.5 but noted its stated scope is life insurance and
  annuities. So what is the P&C equivalent, if any? If there is none, say so
  plainly — that is a useful answer.
- **Does California require a licensed agent to display a licence number** on a
  website that advertises insurance? Quote the rule if one exists. Our page does
  not currently show one.
- Is there any requirement I am missing entirely — disclosure, retention of
  substantiation, anything else?

## Job 2 — Attack the page's specific claims

For each of these, tell me whether a regulator or a competitor could fairly call
it untrue, deceptive, or misleading, and if so what wording would fix it:

1. The headline comparison: **NFIP median $1,244 against $822 for private policies
   we placed**, both at $250,000 building / $5,000 deductible.
2. My framing that the gap is **"an upper bound on what shopping might save you,
   not a prediction"**, because we place private policies only when they beat the
   federal quote. **Is that disclosure sufficient, or does putting the two numbers
   in one table imply a comparison the caveat cannot undo?** This is the question I
   am least comfortable about.
3. The claim that **flood zone does not set your price** under Risk Rating 2.0, and
   my zone table showing A zones at $1,246 against X/B/C at $1,082.
4. "**These are not quotes**" as a disclaimer — modelled on how the California
   Department of Insurance labels its own premium surveys. Is that the right model,
   and is my version strong enough?
5. The **methodology section**. Does it actually discharge a disclosure duty, or
   does it merely look thorough? What is missing that a regulator would expect?
6. The statement that **the NFIP must accept every applicant, so its rates carry
   risks private insurers declined.** Fair characterisation, or disparagement of a
   federal programme?

## Job 3 — What would you have caught that I did not?

Read it as someone trying to get me in trouble. Where is the weakest sentence?

If your answer is that the page is compliant as written, **say so plainly** and
tell me which single element you are least confident about. A clean bill of health
is a useful result, but only if you actually looked for the problem first.

## Ground rules

- **Quote exactly, with URLs.** I check everything, and I have caught errors in
  previous reports from other models.
- Say **"not verified"** rather than giving a plausible answer. A wrong citation on
  a compliance question is worse than a gap.
- **Statute and regulation text beats commentary.** Law-firm blog posts explaining
  what a rule means are not a substitute for the rule.
- You are not my lawyer and I am not treating this as legal advice — I am using it
  to decide what to take to one. Flag anything you think genuinely needs a
  California insurance attorney rather than a research pass.
- **Disagreeing with me is the useful outcome.** If the page should come down until
  it is rewritten, say that.
