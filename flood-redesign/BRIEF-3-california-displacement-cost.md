# Brief 3 — what a California flood displacement actually costs

For Grok. Paste everything below the line.

---

## Why I am asking

NFIP flood policies do not cover temporary housing or additional living expenses.
Private flood policies often do. I surveyed 44 pages that rank for loss-of-use
queries: 41 mention the gap, only 5 put any number on it, and **nobody anywhere
prices a California displacement**. That is the hole I want to fill.

So I need to answer, with sources a reader can check: *if my house floods in
California, where do I live, for how long, and what does that cost me out of
pocket?*

I am not asking you to estimate insurance prices — I have my own data for that.
I am asking for the cost of **housing and living somewhere else**, which is public
information.

## Job 1 — What does temporary housing cost in California?

Give me **monthly cost of a comparable rental** for a displaced single-family
household, for these areas:

- Sacramento County
- San Joaquin County (Stockton)
- Sonoma County (Santa Rosa / Russian River)
- Los Angeles County
- Orange County
- San Diego County
- Santa Clara County
- Monterey County (Pajaro / Salinas Valley)

For each, I want:

- **HUD Fair Market Rent** for a 2-bedroom and a 3-bedroom, current fiscal year,
  from huduser.gov. This is the anchor I most want because it is official,
  published, per-county, and updated annually — I can cite it directly.
- A **market rent** figure from a second source (Zillow Observed Rent Index,
  RentCafe, Apartment List — name which), so I can show the official figure is
  conservative.
- State the **fiscal year or month** each figure belongs to. A rent number without
  a date is useless to me.

Then, separately: is there published data on **short-term or furnished rental**
costs in California? A displaced family usually cannot sign a 12-month lease, and
month-to-month furnished housing costs more than FMR. If there is a credible
published premium for that — a percentage or a dollar figure — give it with the
source. If there is not, say so plainly.

## Job 2 — How long are people actually displaced after a flood?

This is the number that turns a monthly rent into a real figure, and it is the one
I trust least. Find **published, sourced** answers on how long a household is out
of a flood-damaged home before returning:

- Anything from FEMA, the NFIP, state OES, or a university disaster-research
  centre on **median or typical repair and reoccupancy time** after residential
  flooding
- Any figures specific to recent California events — the **2023 Pajaro flood**, the
  **2023 Planada flood (Merced County)**, the **2017 Russian River floods**, or the
  **2019 Guerneville flood**. News reporting is acceptable here if it is specific
  and dated; tell me it is journalism rather than research.
- If the honest answer is "there is no good published median," **say that**. I
  would rather publish "here is what one month costs, multiply by your own
  situation" than invent a duration.

## Job 3 — the question that could sink the whole argument

If FEMA disaster assistance already pays for temporary housing, my argument is
much weaker than I think. I need to know this before I publish, not after.

- Does **FEMA Individual Assistance** cover temporary housing / rental assistance
  after a flood? Quote the program description from fema.gov.
- **What has to happen first?** My understanding is that IA is only available after
  a Presidential major disaster declaration that specifically includes Individual
  Assistance. Confirm or correct that, with a source.
- **How often does that actually happen in California?** How many California flood
  events in, say, the last ten years received a major disaster declaration *with*
  Individual Assistance? A count with a source list is ideal.
- What are the **caps and limits** on IA housing assistance — the maximum award for
  the current fiscal year, and how long assistance lasts?
- Critically: **if my house floods and there is no declaration, what does FEMA give
  me?** I believe the answer is nothing, but I want it sourced.

Tell me straight if this weakens the loss-of-use argument. A finding I do not like
is worth more to me than one I do.

## Ground rules

- **Quote exactly, with URLs.** I check everything, and I have caught errors in
  previous reports.
- Say **"not verified"** rather than giving a plausible answer. A number I later
  find is wrong, after it is on a public page with my licence number on it, costs
  me far more than a gap.
- **Date every figure.** Rents and award caps change annually.
- Distinguish **official data** from **journalism** from **industry marketing**. A
  figure from an insurance company's blog is a claim, not a source.
- Do not estimate or model insurance premiums.
