How much does flood insurance cost in Florida?

Across 342 Florida properties we quoted between February 2025 and August 2026, the median all-in private flood premium was $681 a year. The middle half fall between $492 and $984 — but the tails are the widest we see anywhere: the cheapest tenth come in under $472, and the most expensive tenth run past $1,643. “All-in” means premium plus policy fee and Florida surplus lines taxes.

Where the federal programme runs out

The NFIP’s residential building limit is $250,000. It is statutory — Congress sets it, and no underwriter can raise it. Twenty-one percent of the Florida properties we quote carry building limits above that ceiling, the highest share of any state in our book, against 16% in Texas and 9% in Arizona.

For those homes a federal policy is not merely less generous; it is structurally incapable of covering the building. A $500,000 Florida home on an NFIP policy is insured for half its rebuild cost, and no amount of shopping the federal programme changes that. For roughly a fifth of the Florida market, private is the only route to being made whole.

The widest spread in the country

Florida’s tenth-to-ninetieth percentile range runs from $472 to $1,643 — a 3.5× spread, and the widest of any state we write. A state median is a genuinely poor guide to your own address here.

That range is geography. Florida contains inland Zone X neighbourhoods with shallow rainfall exposure and coastal AE properties facing storm surge, sitting a few miles apart. At $895, Florida Zone AE sits at the top of our national book alongside Texas at $892 — the two are effectively tied — and well above California at $763, because Gulf and Atlantic surge is genuinely deeper and more frequent than most of what the model prices elsewhere.

Most Florida buyers are outside the floodplain

Fifty-eight percent of the Florida properties we quote sit in Zone X, where no federal rule requires cover. Most private flood insurance here is bought voluntarily — which fits a state where FEMA reports 29% of NFIP claims nationally come from outside high-risk flood areas (FEMA, FloodSmart.gov), and where hurricane rainfall routinely floods homes the maps call low risk.

Our Florida book is unusually evenly spread — Naples, Pensacola, Miramar, Vero Beach and Edgewater lead, with no single metro dominating. That reflects the simple fact that Florida’s flood exposure is statewide rather than concentrated in one basin. Nine in ten Florida quotes carry a $5,000 deductible, and around nine in ten are primary residences.

Private flood insurance often runs 10–30% (and in many cases 30–50%) below the NFIP rate for homes with favorable characteristics — newer construction, elevation above base flood elevation, or moderate-risk zones.
