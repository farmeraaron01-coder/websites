How much does flood insurance cost in Texas?

Across 367 Texas properties we quoted between February 2025 and August 2026, the median all-in private flood premium was $670 a year. The middle half of quotes fall between $509 and $970, the cheapest tenth under $469, and the most expensive tenth above $1,272. “All-in” means premium plus policy fee and Texas surplus lines taxes — the figure on the bill, not a base rate.

Most Texas flood insurance is bought voluntarily

Fifty-eight percent of the Texas properties we quote sit in Zone X — outside the mapped high-risk floodplain, where no federal rule requires cover. These are people buying flood insurance because they decided to, not because a lender told them to. That is a very different market from the inland states we write, where high-risk zones dominate.

It is also the correct read of Texas flood risk. Harvey flooded tens of thousands of homes outside the 100-year floodplain in 2017; the map is a regulatory boundary, not a promise. FEMA reports that from 2014 to 2024, 29% of NFIP claims came from areas outside high-risk flood zones (FEMA, FloodSmart.gov).

The $278 zone gap

Texas Zone AE prices at $892 against Zone X at $614 — one of the widest zone spreads in our national book, and among the most expensive AE anywhere we write. The reason is depth: Texas coastal and bayou AE zones carry genuine surge and deep-riverine exposure, and under Risk Rating 2.0 that is priced on the building’s actual vulnerability rather than averaged across the zone. Where the hazard is real, the price reflects it.

Where in Texas we quote

Houston is our largest Texas market by a wide margin, followed by Cypress, Spring, Katy and Pearland. That clustering is not an accident of marketing — Harris County and its neighbours combine flat terrain, heavy clay soils, rapid development and extreme rainfall, and the result is a metropolitan area where properties well outside the floodplain still flood.

About 16% of the Texas properties we quote need more than $250,000 of building coverage, which is the NFIP’s statutory ceiling and cannot be raised by any underwriter. For those homes the private market is not a cheaper alternative; it is the only route to being fully covered. Nine in ten Texas quotes carry a $5,000 deductible, and nearly nine in ten are primary residences.
