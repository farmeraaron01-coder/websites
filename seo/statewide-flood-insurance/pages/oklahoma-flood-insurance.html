Oklahoma is the second least expensive state in our book, behind only Michigan. Across 93 Oklahoma properties quoted between February 2025 and August 2026, the median all-in private flood premium was $465 a year — roughly $226 below the median across the 27 states we track. It also has the widest relative spread of any state we write, and that combination is the whole story of flood insurance here.

What flood insurance costs in Oklahoma

Median all-in cost is $465 a year. The middle half of quotes land between $350 and $675. But the tails run a long way in both directions: the cheapest tenth come in under $302, and the most expensive tenth run past $1,109. That is a 3.7× gap between the tenth and ninetieth percentile — wider, proportionally, than any other state in our book.

Why Oklahoma prices low

The single biggest reason is building value. The median building limit on an Oklahoma quote in our book is $190,000 — the lowest of any state we write, and well under the $250,000 that is standard almost everywhere else. Flood premium scales with what it costs to rebuild, so a state of modestly valued housing prices low almost by definition.

We do not publish zone-level medians for Oklahoma. Our zone samples here run between 20 and 47 properties, and a median from a sample that size moves too much to stand behind as a price.

The tail is the real story

A median of $465 makes Oklahoma sound uniformly cheap. It is not. One Oklahoma property in ten prices above $1,109 — more than double the median — and about 12% need building coverage above the NFIP’s $250,000 statutory cap.

That is what a flash-flood state looks like in pricing data. Oklahoma’s hazard is intense, short-duration rainfall over creeks and urban drainage rather than seasonal river rise, and exposure varies enormously street to street. Two houses a few hundred yards apart can price very differently because one sits a few feet lower or closer to a channel. A state median tells you almost nothing about your own address here, which is a stronger argument for getting an actual quote than in a state where everything clusters.

Where in Oklahoma we quote

Our Oklahoma book is spread across Midwest City, Oklahoma City, Tulsa, Broken Arrow and Duncan — metropolitan and small-town alike, with no single city dominating. The two metros between them account for well under a quarter of the properties we price, which reflects how widely distributed Oklahoma’s flood exposure actually is.

What drives your Oklahoma premium

Nine in ten Oklahoma quotes carry a $5,000 deductible. Around eight in ten properties we price are primary homes, with rentals and second homes splitting the remainder evenly.

Beyond that, the rating model prices your building: first-floor height, distance to the nearest creek or drainage channel, foundation type, replacement cost, prior claims and occupancy. Under Risk Rating 2.0 the zone letter sets whether your lender requires cover; it does not set your price.

Frequently asked questions

Is flood insurance required in Oklahoma?
Only with a federally backed mortgage on a property in Zone A or AE. There is no state requirement, and no federal rule applies in Zone X — though a lender may impose its own condition.

Why is Oklahoma cheaper than other states?
Lower rebuild costs, principally. The median building limit we quote in Oklahoma is $190,000 against $250,000 in most states, and premium follows replacement cost.

Does homeowners insurance cover flash flooding?
No. Every standard homeowners policy in the United States excludes flood, including flash flooding from creeks and storm drains. It is always a separate policy. Wind-driven rain entering through a damaged roof is a different peril and may be covered — the distinction matters after a severe-weather event.

Is Zone X worth insuring in Oklahoma?
Often, yes. Flash flooding over creeks and urban drainage is exactly the hazard that reaches homes outside the mapped floodplain, and FEMA reports that from 2014 to 2024, 29% of NFIP claims came from outside high-risk flood areas (FEMA, FloodSmart.gov). With a statewide median of $465, cover in Oklahoma is inexpensive relative to most of the country.
