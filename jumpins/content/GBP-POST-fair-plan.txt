===== POST TEXT — copy from the next line =====

California has approved a 29.1% average rate increase for the FAIR Plan, effective October 15, 2026 — on renewals and new policies alike.

If you're one of the 668,600+ California households on the FAIR Plan, two things matter before then.

First: 29.1% is a statewide average, not your increase. The new rates are set using catastrophe models, so the increase concentrates where wildfire risk is highest. Some homes go well past 29% and some wildfire premiums roughly double. Lower-exposure homes see less, and a minority will see a decrease. You won't know your number until the renewal arrives.

Second — and this is the part worth acting on — the regular market has reopened. California's Sustainable Insurance Strategy requires carriers that want to use catastrophe models to commit to writing in wildfire areas, and the list of companies accepting new California homeowners business is growing for the first time since 2022.

So a home pushed onto the FAIR Plan during the 2023-2025 pullback may now have real options. In San Diego County the risk runs inland and east, and two homes a few miles apart can price very differently.

Don't wait for the renewal notice. Quoting can require an inspection and takes time to bind. Starting now beats an October renewal comfortably; starting in October does not.

If you carry a companion policy alongside the FAIR Plan, bring both declarations pages. Comparing the FAIR Plan premium by itself is how people wrongly conclude they can't do better.

WHAT NOT TO USE
───────────────
Do not use the three wildfire images in the media library:

  firefighters-california-wildfire.jpg
  altadena-wildfire-damage-california.jpg
  wildfire_homeowners_insurance1.jpg

Two reasons. The Altadena photograph is real destroyed homes, and using it
to sell rate shopping is the kind of thing people notice and resent. The
other two are dramatic flame shots — one appears to be a fuel-fire training
exercise rather than a wildfire at all.

The bigger problem is that they argue the wrong case. This post is not about
fire. It is about a rate, a deadline, and the fact that there is now a way
off the FAIR Plan. Flames say "be afraid"; the post says "you have options
and about ten weeks". Fear imagery on a profile that is asking for a phone
call reads as fear-selling, and it undercuts the calm, factual tone that
makes the post credible in the first place.

The rule the attached photo follows: show the thing being insured, not the
thing that destroys it.

Worth trying instead on a future post — a photograph of the San Diego office
or the team. Real people outperform generated and stock imagery on GBP, and
it reinforces that this is a local agency. Nothing suitable exists in the
media library today; the only calm images there are low-resolution house
illustrations from 2021.

No carrier names. The article names Mercury, CSAA, Farmers and Travelers as
